What 100 Flagged Cases Taught Me About Content Moderation

Every day I review hundreds of flagged pieces of content — and no two cases are the same. What surprised me most wasn't the volume, it was the patterns. Harmful content rarely announces itself. It hides in context, in tone, in what's not said.

Over time I learned that great moderation isn't just about following rules — it's about reading intent. A post can be technically within guidelines but still cause real harm. Another can look suspicious and turn out completely innocent. Judgment matters more than checklists.

I also discovered something unexpected: moderation makes you a better content creator. When you've spent months understanding what makes content harmful or inauthentic, you apply those same filters to everything you create. Protecting people online requires the same empathy you'd want shown to you offline.
